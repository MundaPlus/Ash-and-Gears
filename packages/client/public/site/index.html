The idea

RimWorld's people. Factorio's machines. The end of the world.

You do not control anyone directly. You give orders, mark zones and set priorities. Your survivors decide the rest, and they have opinions.

Every survivor is a story

Names, traits, skills, moods and bodies that break. Sulks, lost limbs, and the occasional hero.

From scrap to machines

Start with bare hands. Research power, belts and auto-crafters, and let the machines take over the chores.

The wasteland pushes back

Raiders, rad-rats, glow-hounds at night, fire, and radiation clouds that poison what you leave outside.

Laugh so you don't cry

Cold beans from a can: +1 mood. Raw mutant meat: −9. The writing is grim and a little funny.

Build

Orders, not puppets

Drag a room. Watch it happen.

Mark walls, floors and beds. Survivors haul the scrap and wood, build, and move in. Closed rooms get a roof and keep the heat in.

  • Salvage ruins and wrecks, mine underground, cut trees
  • Rooms with temperature, beds you own, stockpiles
  • Work priorities for every survivor
Fight

Draft and defend

Raiders want your beans.

Draft survivors, take cover, and shoot back. Turrets help once you have power. Doctors drag the wounded to bed and patch them up.

  • Hit chance from skill, range and cover
  • Body parts, bleeding, downed survivors
  • A storyteller that paces the trouble
Radiation

Weather with teeth

The cloud is coming. Move the food inside.

You get half a day of warning. Survivors run for cover. Crops, water and food left under the open sky stay radioactive for a whole season.

  • Sealed rooms keep supplies clean
  • Wells inside the walls stay safe
  • Lamps and campfires light up the night
Automate

Progressive automation

Belts first. Then the machines run themselves.

Research scrap power, wire up generators and batteries, and feed an auto-crafter with a conveyor line. One project needs a manual you have to find in an old safe.

  • Power networks with brownouts
  • Loaders, belts and auto-crafters
  • Research tree with salvaged blueprints

New in the Alpha

Deeper machines. Deeper people. A whole world outside.

Nine milestones since the demo: belt logistics, pipes and fluids, a real power grid, tech tiers 2 and 3 with an old bunker underground, traits, backstories and relationships, prisoners, robots, a world map with expeditions, and factions that trade or raid.

Salvage Print

The look

Salvage Print: a worn two-color print.

The new default style is drawn in code: a few flat inks, halftone dots for shade, color a little off the outline, and a wobbly ink line. It stays sharp at every zoom. People have four facings, arms that hold their tools, and packs on their backs. The hand-pixeled 16 px style stays as an option in Settings.

  • Things from straight above, with printed shadows
  • Three ink sets: Rust, Ash and Toxic
  • Doors, belts, turbines, batteries and benches animate
Logistics

Belts, pipes, power

Pump the sludge. Purify it. Pipe it to a tap.

Splitters, sorters, underground belts, robot arms and chests move items between machines. Pipes carry water and fuel. Poles, switches, sun and wind make a real grid, and when power runs short the low-priority machines go off first.

  • Machines take ingredients on one side and give products on the other
  • Pumps, tanks, purifiers, taps and a fuel still
  • Power overlay with the P key
Robots

A workforce with limits

Robots haul, build and craft. They will not save your life.

Build robots and hauler drones at a robot bay. They never get hungry or sad, but they run on charge, go back to the bay to recharge and get repaired, fight badly, and never doctor.

  • Robot bay builds, charges and repairs
  • Hauler drones only haul, but fast
  • Survivors become managers and fighters
Deep

Tech tiers 2–3

Dig down to the old bunker.

Research deep digging and dig stairs from the caves. A sealed bunker waits below with old-world lockers. Some hold the manuals you need for plastics, electronics and the assembler.

  • Refinery: fuel to plastic
  • Electronics from parts and plastic
  • The assembler, a fast auto-crafter
People

Survivors with history

Friends, rivals, lovers. And prisoners.

Thirty traits and 24 backstories shape every survivor. They talk, argue, fall in love and grieve. Capture downed raiders, give them a bed, and a warden can talk them into joining you.

  • Opinions from talks, rescues and raids survived together
  • Doctors rush to a downed friend
  • Prisoners, wardens, recruiting, release
World

The world outside

Send a team. Hope they come back.

A world map with ruins, bunkers, wreck fields, dead towns and a crater. Pack food and water, send survivors to scavenge, and found outposts that gather while you are away. Rust Knights and the Glow Cult raid you; the Water Union and Scrap Nomads come to trade.

  • Expeditions with travel, loot and ambushes
  • Outposts that gather every day
  • Factions with goodwill, caravans and gifts

New in the Beta

Pick a story. Play it to the end.

The Beta gives the colony a goal and an ending: three storylines, five start scenarios, specialist robots, mods, and a long list of changes that make the colony feel alive. Every survivor now relaxes, sleeps on a schedule, and buries the dead.

Story

Three storylines

Restart the reactor. Fix the bus. Call the sky.

Each story is a chain of chapters with goals. Story places appear on the world map with parts you cannot find anywhere else. A rusty safety robot, a radio ad for a safe valley, and voices at night lead the way.

  • Keep the Lights On: HAL-PH, five reactor parts, the Glow Cult
  • The Last Bus: rebuild a school bus and drive to Haven Valley
  • Is Anyone Up There?: relays, a dish and a price to pay
Endings

Every run ends

A slide for each survivor. Or an obituary.

Win, and every survivor gets a line made from what they did: the fighter, the builder, the one who mostly supervised. Lose everyone, and the game writes an obituary. After a win you can keep playing.

  • Lines from kills, rescues, builds, crafts and traits
  • Each storyline has its own music cue and chapter banner
  • Five start scenarios: Sandbox, Lone Wanderer, Mall Rats, The Bunker, Rad Season
Robots

Tier 4

Builders, miners, farmers, guards. And hackers.

The robot bay now builds specialists. Guard robots fight on their own when enemies come close. Later raiders carry hacking gear and can turn one of your robots against you for half a day.

  • The bay stores power like two batteries and charges robots from it
  • Charge bars in red, yellow and green over every robot and battery
  • Every machine has a small backup battery for blackouts
Life

A colony that lives

Fun, fire, wood and graves.

Survivors get bored and relax at the campfire, a card table or a horseshoe pit. Fires and stoves burn wood, so you plant ash willows. The dead must be buried, or the living will not stop thinking about them.

  • A fun need and three ways to relax
  • Fuel, charge and work progress shown on the map
  • Food spoils visibly; work in the dark is slower
Control

More control

Schedules, stockpile priority, haul now.

Paint each survivor's day: sleep, work, recreation or anything. Mark a stack "haul now" and it goes first. Stockpiles have priorities, so food moves to the kitchen and steel to the factory.

  • Tile actions on the left, default on the F key
  • Alerts you can click to jump to the problem
  • The top bar counts only what is stored
Mods

Make it yours

Mods are plain JSON.

A mod is one file in the same shape as the game's own content: items, buildings, recipes, research, stories, strings and 16 px art. Add it from the title screen. A broken pack is left out, with errors that say what is wrong.

  • New ids add content; known ids replace it
  • Load order: later mods win
  • An example pack and a short guide come with the game

Pick the look

Two styles. Three palettes.

Salvage Print is the default. The hand-pixeled 16 px style below is the other option: every sprite on a 16-pixel grid with shared color roles, so the whole game can switch palettes live. Switch styles and palettes in the game menu under Settings.
